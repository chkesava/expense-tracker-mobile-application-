# Audit Log — Master Index

This directory tracks a running series of codebase audits for the
expense-tracker app. By default each phase is diagnostic only — issues are
logged here in full, with no shortcuts taken in the write-up, and no fixes
are applied during an audit phase unless separately requested. A phase may be
explicitly scoped as an audit-**and**-hardening pass (as Phase 2 was); when
that happens, the phase report says so up front and still logs every issue
in full before describing what was fixed vs. left open. This index is
updated every time a new phase is completed.

## Conventions

- One file per phase: `docs/audits/PHASE_N_<short-topic>.md`.
- Every issue in a phase report must include: what the issue is, why it
  matters, and a concrete failure scenario — not a one-line label.
- Every issue is ranked **P0** (must fix before next release / actively
  dangerous) / **P1** (should fix soon, meaningful risk) / **P2** (should fix,
  moderate impact) / **P3** (worth fixing, low urgency).
- Each phase report ends with a recommended fix order.
- When a later phase re-checks an issue from an earlier phase, it must
  re-verify current file/state before calling it "still open," "fixed," or
  "changed" — never assume a prior finding still holds without looking again.
- This index lists every phase, its date, its scope, and its open P0/P1 count
  at time of writing.

- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** A stored `billingPeriodStart` was trusted verbatim, and statements written before the close-on-D fix used the *previous* generation day rather than the day after — so consecutive windows shared that boundary day and spend on it was billed in two statements (500 on 1 Jul + 1,000 on 15 Jul + 156 on 1 Aug came out as 2,156 billed against 1,656 of spend). The UI tell was a past cycle reading "2026-07-01 -> 2026-08-01", 32 days with both ends on a generation day. A stored window may no longer reach outside the cycle it closes: start clamped forward to the derived cycle start, end clamped back to the statement date, a deliberately narrower window still respected. Unpaid auto statements self-heal on next open; PAID ones are left alone by design.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** Reverts the netting half of the entry below. Subtracting unmatched credit from `totalOutstanding` and flooring at 0 meant a card with 393 of real unbilled spend and 7,497 of unmatched credit reported "Total outstanding 0" — hiding a real debt behind a phantom credit. And the credit was never an advance: it is the residue of a payment recorded against spend the app never saw (19,000 paid where the statements it could settle came to 11,503), so labelling it "Already paid in advance" asserted something untrue about the user's money. `unappliedCredit` is now gone entirely — no field, no row, no netting — and credit that outlives every debt is dropped rather than carried. The payment cap is kept, which is what makes dropping safe: unmatched credit can no longer accrue from normal use. Accepted cost, raised before the change: a genuine overpayment is not credited back in net worth.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** `totalOutstanding` never subtracted `unappliedCredit` while `useUnifiedNetWorth` consumed `totalOutstanding`, so a card holding money paid in advance reported the user poorer by exactly that amount. The advance is now netted out of outstanding, the hero row relabelled from the jargon "Credit balance" to "Already paid in advance", and the pay-bill modal caps a payment at what the card owes so no new advance accrues by accident.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** Leftover payment credit was applied to open-cycle spend regardless of which cycle the payment was made in, so credit left over from a payment made *before* a statement closed silently absorbed spend charged *after* it — a card whose statement cut on the 21st showed "Unbilled (this cycle) 0" with a real 393 charge on the 22nd sitting on it, netted away against a 7,497 credit balance. Leftover is now bucketed by cycle: credit paid inside the open cycle may reduce that cycle's spend, credit carried from an earlier cycle is a standing balance reported as `unappliedCredit`. Reported from the device after the audit; the audit's own hunt list did not ask whether credit leaks across a close date, and the existing tests covered leftover-with-open-cycle and spend-after-close separately but never both at once.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** The stored `amountPaid` floor in `buildCreditCardLedger` had no date guard and no provenance check, so on a non-auto (or PAID) statement it credited money that allocation had deliberately withheld because the payment predated the statement — while the same money also stayed in `freeCredit`. A ₹19,000 payment dated 13 Aug against a ₹28,101 statement closing 20 Aug showed the statement as PARTIALLY PAID with ₹9,101 remaining *and* kept ₹19,000 as unapplied credit, understating the card liability in net worth by ₹19,000. The floor now covers out-of-band settlements only (the part of `amountPaid` no linked payment explains) and never applies to a statement that has not closed yet.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** The manual statement form (`CreateCreditCardBillModal.handleSubmit`) validated nothing at all: `parseFloat` on an empty or non-numeric field passed `NaN` as the statement amount into `createBill`, and a statement could be dated in the future or given a billing window running past its own close date. Hand-entered statements feed the same ledger as generated ones, so these corrupt the card's position rather than just looking odd. Added `validateCreditCardBillInput` (pure, unit-tested): card selected, amount > 0, minimum due within the statement, real dates, statement date not in the future, due date on/after it, and a billing period bounded by the close date.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** `magicParser.test.ts` built "yesterday" from `toISOString()` (UTC) and compared it to a parser that resolves relative dates on the local calendar, so it failed every day from 18:30 IST onward — a permanently red test that masked real breakage. Now freezes local noon and asserts a literal date key.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** Found while fixing the above: in `magicParser.ts` the relative-date patterns are matched in array order with a break on first hit, and `/\byesterday\b/` sat before `/\bday before yesterday\b/` — which the shorter pattern matches inside. "day before yesterday" therefore resolved to one day back instead of two, and left "day before" polluting the extracted note. Reordered longest-first.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** `computeCreditUsage()` in `accountBalance.ts` defined available credit as `limit - gross cycle spend`, ignoring statements, leftover credit and the ledger entirely. No UI still called it, but three tests did — and one asserted its wrong answer (5,000 spend fully settled by 6,500 of payments reported 5,000 used / 95,000 available on a card that actually had the full 100,000 free plus 1,500 of credit sitting on it). Deleted, with the three call sites migrated to `computeOutstandingCredit`.
- **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** Spend under a CANCELLED statement fell back into `unbilledSpend`, which is what `availableCredit` subtracts, so cancelling a statement quietly reduced the available limit by spend from an already-closed cycle — contradicting the model's rule that available credit is the limit minus *this cycle's* unbilled spend. The spend is still owed (unchanged intent) but now sits in its own `cancelledSpend` bucket: counted in `totalOutstanding`, excluded from `availableCredit`. - **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** Auto statement generation only ever drafted the *latest* closed cycle, so a user who did not open the app for two or more cycles never got statement documents (or reminders) for the cycles they missed — the ledger already derived and billed those windows, so the position was right but the documents were absent. `collectAutoCreditCardBillDrafts` now backfills the last 12 closed cycles (matching the ledger's derived-cycle depth), skipping cycles with no spend, and the refresh pass repairs the backfilled documents too. - **[Credit card revalidation 2026-08-22]** `PayCreditBillModal` picked its payment target by `dueDate` with no date guard and stamped the **full** payment amount onto it, so a backdated payment or a manually created future-dated statement wrote `amountPaid > 0` onto a statement the payment cannot settle, and an overpayment wrote `amountPaid > statementAmount` (rendered raw as "Amount paid" on the bill detail screen).
